# 0023 — The globe is by language

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-09

## Context

Pageviews carry a language edition, not a reader location. Mapping attention to countries would claim knowledge we
don't have: Spanish readers in the US, English readers in India and German readers in Austria all look the same.

## Decision

- `config/language_geo.yml` maps each of our 30 languages to the countries where it is **official or the majority
  language**.
  - It is generated by `python -m lookedup.language_geo` from Wikidata P37, for current countries with an
    ISO 3166-1 alpha-3 code.
  - A hand check follows. Its additions and removals are listed with reasons in the module, so the file can be
    regenerated.
- **Hand check (2026-10-09):**
  - Added: USA (en, P37 missing); China and Taiwan (zh, P37 points to unmapped Chinese items); Uruguay and Puerto
    Rico (es); Afghanistan (fa: Dari is Persian); Kyrgyzstan (ru); Montenegro (sr, plurality).
  - Removed: Palau (ja, official in one state only); USA and Antigua (es); Afghanistan and Eritrea (ar).
  - India and Pakistan stay under English. It is official there, and they are among English Wikipedia's largest
    audiences.
- **Result:** English spans 63 countries, French 28, Arabic 23, Spanish 21, Portuguese 10. Thirteen languages map
  to one country (e.g. Hebrew, Finnish, Czech, Japanese).
- **App use.** The app reads `app/data/language_geo.json`, keyed by ISO numeric to match world-atlas geometry. It
  colours a country by the attention in its languages for the selected event. Multi-language countries take the max.
- **The UI says it plainly:** "We see languages, not countries."

## Consequences

- Countries whose main languages are outside our 30 stay grey. Examples: most of sub-Saharan Africa outside the
  English, French and Portuguese areas, and Central Asia.
- English's breadth paints a third of the map whenever English spikes. The legend explains this.
